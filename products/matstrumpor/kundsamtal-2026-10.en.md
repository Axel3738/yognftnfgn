## Pattern so far (n = 3, confirm before building on it)

1. **All three buy as a gift for someone younger who loves sushi; two of three
   buy for grandchildren.** Our DNA avatar is "woman 30–55 buying for mum,
   brother or partner". The repeat buyers who answered are older and buy
   downwards in the family. The buyer doesn't even eat sushi (Görel).
   Hypothesis to test: **grandparent giving to the grandchild who loves sushi.**
2. **Nobody talks about the socks. Everyone talks about the box and the
   reaction.** "Cool gift box", "the box is so pretty", a pizza box to wrap it
   in, "never seen anything like it". The product is the moment the box opens.
3. **Zero objections in three of three.** Not price, not quality, not delivery.
   Repeat buying stops when they run out of people to give to, not because
   anything stops them.
4. **The word itself is a hook.** "Sushi socks!" as a mood-breaker in a group
   of friends is the best line in the material. It's true, and no competitor
   can write it.
