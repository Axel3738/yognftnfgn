## 1. Cost caps — what Evolve actually does

Spencer is blunt: "doesn't matter — We run 1 chad CBO because ad creatives matter more" [C1]. Shaun
confirms the default at scale: "majority of our spend in high spending accounts is still lowest
cost" [C2]. Neither mentions Andromeda or the inflated-budget method from the video.

The only structured use of cost caps in the community is Spencer's zombie campaign: ads that didn't
get spend in your CBO within 7 days get dripped into a cost-cap campaign and left to chill [C3]. It's
a salvage tool, not a testing or scaling structure.

Shaun explicitly says "I wouldn't test with CC" — cost caps are not for launching new creatives [C3].
His guidance on scaling winners is to just increase budget on what's already working (ABO or CBO),
not restructure into capped campaigns [C4].

One member, habibi, described a cost-cap setup: 10 adsets at $5 over and $5 under break-even CPA,
with winning adsets graduated into a single scaling CBO [C6]. No one validated or endorsed this in
the conversation — it's a member's own method, not community consensus.

On cannibalization risk if you do test cost caps alongside your main CBO: Shanil's view is that if
the cost-cap campaign uses different creatives, it shouldn't cannibalize [C5]. Same creatives = risk.

No source gives a minimum spend threshold for cost caps, a specific day count for judging them, or
any post-Andromeda data.

**Bottom line on Q1:** Don't break your working highest-volume CBO. If you want to experiment, the
only community-endorsed cost-cap use is the zombie campaign for no-spend ads. The video's approach
isn't validated by anything in the community's data.

## 2. Customer surveys — already covered in this thread

This was addressed in detail earlier. The key points: Spencer prefers open-ended written answers
("people actually have to type shit") over multiple choice, because you can see who took it
seriously [from earlier thread answer citing Spencer's CRO framework]. Thank-you page placement
works; post-delivery surveys got zero responses (Chrisphillips tried it — "they never did that
survey"). On incentives, Spencer prefers sweepstakes over per-response rewards for short surveys. The
purpose is to surface non-obvious avatars and desires — Karos's example where surveys revealed the
real buyer was overweight men, not pregnant women, is the canonical example in the community.

No source provides exact question wording, response rates, or incentive-bias data.

## 3. Organic TikTok/Reels

One data point: Idris started organically (160k IG followers), and 2 of his top-spending Meta ads
also crushed organically. His metric is sales per million views — $10k per 1M views is good;
gimmicky/viral content that gets views but converts at $1k per 1M views doesn't work as ads [C7].

Key caveat from Idris: "sometimes Meta just won't spend on something that performed well on
organic" — organic success doesn't guarantee paid success [C7]. Organic rewards engaging hooks; Meta
rewards click-through and conversion intent.

No source directly addresses whether organic is worth the time for a small team with
one-time-purchase products. Idris's brand started organically and built an audience first — your
stores are paid-first with ~1% repeat. The sourcing doesn't cover this decision.
