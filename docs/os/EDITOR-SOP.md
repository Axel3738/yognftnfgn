# Editor SOP — how your work day runs

Written for the video editors. English throughout.

Everything you need is in the dashboard your manager sends you. You never need to
guess what to work on, and you never lose credit for work you finished.

## Where things go in Slack (workspace: stonebite)

| Channel | What goes there |
|---------|-----------------|
| `#rapporter` | **Your end-of-day report. Every workday, before 21:30 CET.** |
| One channel per product | Briefs, questions, blockers, delivery links for that product |
| `#ugc` | UGC outreach only |

Blockers go in the **product channel immediately** when they happen — do not save
them for the end-of-day report.

## The day in five steps

### 1. Find your tasks

Every morning you get a Slack message like this:

> Good morning, Maria. You have 4 tasks today for Axelbältet and Motorhöljet.
> The goal is 6 finished creatives. Start with AXE-013 — it ships first.

Open the link. Your page shows only your own tasks, the highest priority first,
with a progress bar: *"3 of 6 creatives delivered"*.

Each task tells you: the product, the task type, how many creatives to deliver,
the deadline, the brief link, and the folder with source material.

### 2. Start a task

Reply **START** in Slack, or tell your manager. The task moves to *In progress*.

Do this when you actually begin. It is how your manager knows the day is moving,
and it is what makes your delivery-time numbers fair to you.

### 3. Report a blocker — early, not at 5pm

If anything stops you, say so **immediately**. A blocker reported at 10:00 usually
gets solved the same morning. The same blocker reported at 17:00 costs a whole day.

Answer these four questions:
1. **What is blocking you?**
2. **Which task?**
3. **What do you need to continue?**
4. **Who needs to act?**

Example:
> Blocked on AXE-008. The Drive folder linked in the brief has no voice-over file.
> I need the VO uploaded, or approval to use the old one. Anna needs to decide.

Reporting a blocker is never held against you. Reporting it late is the problem.

### 4. Deliver your work

When a task is finished:
1. Upload the files to the delivery folder from the brief.
2. Use the exact file names from the naming convention.
3. Export every format the brief asks for (video 9:16 + 4:5, images 1:1 + 1080×1350).
4. Send the **delivery link** and the number of creatives.

**A task is never done without a delivery link.** No link, no delivery.

### 5. Answer the checklist

Before your work goes to your manager, you answer a short checklist. It is not a
test — it exists so nothing gets sent back for a small thing you could have caught.

Always asked:
- Does the delivery link work?
- Correct product?
- Does it match the brief?
- Correct file names?
- Correct aspect ratios?
- Clean export — no watermark, audio correct?
- Previous feedback implemented?

For a new creative you also answer, in your own words:
- **Which hook did you use for each version?**
- **What is the main angle?**
- **What is actually different between the versions?**

Answer that last one honestly. Three versions with a different text colour are not
three creatives — they are one. Real variations are what your manager needs, and
what the ad account rewards.

### 6. End-of-day report

**Where: `#rapporter` in the stonebite Slack. One message, once per day, before
21:30 CET.** Not in the product channels — those are for briefs and questions.
One report per person per day, even if you worked on several products.

Copy this template every day:

```
RAPPORT <date> — <your name>

DONE
- <TASK-ID> — <n> creatives — <delivery link>
- <TASK-ID> — <n> creatives — <delivery link>

NOT DONE
- <TASK-ID> — <why, in one line>

BLOCKED
- <TASK-ID> — <what you need, and who has to act>   (or: none)

TOMORROW
- <what you start with>
```

If you finished nothing, send the report anyway with `DONE: none` and the reason.
A missing report reads as a missing day in your numbers — an empty report does not.

Plain language inside the fields is fine — the system reads it and matches it to
your tasks. It will show you what it understood and ask you to confirm before
anything is saved.

## What happens after you deliver

Your task goes to **Ready for review** — not approved. Your manager checks it and
either approves it or sends it back with feedback.

**Delivered and approved are two different things.** Only approved work counts
towards the product's target, so a task sent back is not finished yet.

## Handling feedback

When something comes back you get the exact reason. Read it, fix it, re-upload to
the same folder, and reply DONE.

The feedback stays attached to the task. Next time you deliver it, the checklist
asks whether you addressed it — so nothing gets lost between rounds.

If feedback is unclear, ask before you start reworking. One question now beats a
second rejected version.

## How you are measured

Your page shows these, and your manager sees them next to the context:

| Number | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| Creatives delivered | How much you shipped |
| On-time delivery rate | Delivered by the deadline |
| First-pass approval rate | Approved with no revision round |
| Revision rate | How often work comes back |
| Open tasks | Your current workload |

**These are not a leaderboard.** They are always read next to how hard your tasks
were — average creatives per task, average revision rounds, and how many blockers
you hit. Someone taking the difficult briefs is expected to have more revisions,
and that is written into the system, not left to memory.

The one thing that always looks bad: marking work as finished when it is not.
Report the blocker instead. Nobody is judged for a blocker they reported in time.
