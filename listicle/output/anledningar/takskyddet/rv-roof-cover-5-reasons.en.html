3. The last cover they owned did more harm than good.

Ask around on any storage lot and someone has a cover story: bungee cords that stretched out by February, plastic hooks that let go in the first storm, a cover that turned into a parachute, and marks along the sides where it rubbed. One bad cover teaches an owner to leave the roof alone, and after that the roof takes every winter with no protection at all, because a cover once failed him. That history is why the straps on this one are what they are. The maker's reason for using woven webbing instead of elastic is the same story: elastic loses its stretch over time, so they don't use it. There are straps on all four sides, adjustable, with hooks that latch under the edge of the body, and two reinforced straps on top of that, which go across the whole roof. The maker also says the straps will not damage the paint. We haven't tested that ourselves, so it stands here as their word, not ours. One thing you won't find here is a wind rating. Nobody has measured one, and a number nobody measured is worse than no number.

4. For that money, they'd rather wrap the whole RV.

It sounds like the obvious upgrade: if you're spending real money on a cover anyway, why not one that covers the sides and the wheels too? The catch is what a full-body cover asks of you every time it goes on. Two people, a wall of fabric hauled over every corner without snagging, and then a whole winter of that fabric flapping against the sides in the wind. Ask the owner of one how many winters it actually went on. This cover takes the opposite bet. It covers the roof only, because the roof is where the rain sits, at the hatches and along the seams, all winter long. One person unfolds it on the roof, hooks the straps under the edge on all four sides, pulls the two reinforced straps across, and climbs down. The sides stay open, and yes, that means the sides stay uncovered. If wrapping the whole rig is what you want, this isn't your product, and we won't pretend it is.

What about solar panels?

The cover goes over them, so they're covered while it's on. If your batteries depend on the panels through the winter, this cover works against you, and we'd rather say so.

It can. The fabric is waterproof, so little air moves under it. A spacer that lifts the cover off the roof is in the works, with no date and no price yet.
