1. You wipe the windshield every morning. You've stopped thinking about it.

Nobody decides to start every morning of the trip with a rag in their hand. Where you sleep there are walls and a roof. Where you drive there's glass, and the windshield is the biggest surface on the whole motorhome with nothing covering it when the night turns cold. The glass cools down, the moisture in the cab settles on it, and in the morning you're standing there with the rag again. It's not that you don't ventilate enough. 2. The curtain hangs on the wrong side of the glass

Most people have already done something about it. A curtain across the cab, a folding sunshade in the windshield, a blanket over the dashboard on the coldest nights. It feels like a fix, and it does help with one thing: nobody can see in. But the fog doesn't care about the curtain. It sits on the glass, and the glass gets just as cold no matter what hangs on the inside of it. The only thing that changes is where the moisture ends up. Now it settles between the curtain and the windshield instead, where you can't reach it without pulling everything aside, and the hem of the curtain gets damp. In the morning you have the same windshield to wipe, with one extra step before you can get to it. What decides whether the glass is dry when you wake up isn't what hangs on the inside. It's whether the glass was allowed to get cold during the night or not.

In November it's the other way around: the glass is cold, the fog is there, and you're standing with the rag. Two different mornings, same cause. The glass took the weather with nothing in between. A cover that sits on the outside of the windshield stops the sun before it reaches the glass, and keeps the cold off it at night. You can keep the cab from heating up.
