--- slide 3: Supervise current processes for adequacy // Analyze existing factory quality control procedures // Develop and implement new processes if lacking // Initial Evaluation
--- slide 4: Managing quality control at the factory level // Setting QC Standards with the Factory // Ensuring products meet defined quality standards // Working with a Sourcing company, hiring a 3rd party inspection company, or having your own in-house employee // Understanding and agreeing on the full quality control process and specs with the factory from IQC, testing and  final assembly // Create a clear reporting process to share defects and complaints with the factory and establish actionable next steps // Quality Control Best Practices
--- slide 5: Manufacturing risk analysis & acceptance criteria- What is the factory’s QC process? // Production supervision- Is the factory using proper processes? What documents are in place to make sure you are getting what you paid for? // Final product inspection- How are you ensuring the final product being packaged is totally acceptable. Establish go-no-go systems. // Approval  to ship- Random checks of a defined amount should be inspected and reported to you before any shipment. Consider using a 3rd party (or our boots on the ground) for unbiased opinions. // Our Quality Control Process // Initial inspection of existing quality control processes
--- slide 6: Risk Analysis and  Production Supervision // Identify and reduce manufacturing risks- make sure QC documents are present and being followed at every step during production. // Define clear quality standards and references- create clear standards with clear go-no-go parameters // Reports end evidence- Ask for photos and videos of each production batch to prove they are following the process, or go there yourself for spot checks
--- slide 7: Conduct final inspection after manufacturing // Check for defects and packaging issues // Provide final approval to ship products // Final Inspection and Shipping
--- slide 8: Failing to thoroughly vet a supplier’s experience, certifications, and track record can result in working with factories that lack the capability or commitment to maintain consistent quality. // Not properly evaluating supplier credibility // Without physically inspecting the factory and production process, quality issues can go unnoticed until after products are manufactured and shipped. // Not performing onsite inspections // Relying entirely on suppliers to police their own quality standards creates a conflict of interest and increases the risk of defects being overlooked or ignored. // Leaving quality control solely to suppliers // Common Mistakes in China Quality Control
--- slide 9: Misunderstandings can happen when factory staff and buyers don’t share the same language, so using clear documentation, visuals, and reliable translators helps prevent errors. // Language and communication barriers // When you aren’t physically present, it’s harder to verify processes are being followed, so onsite visits or trusted third-party inspectors reduce blind spots. When you can’t be present, ask for pictures and videos of the process and keep the communication line open to address any issues you find quickly. // Difficulty in supervising your production remotely // Defining measurable quality standards can be difficult without experience. Specs, tolerances, and checklists can be a foreign concept. Remain patient and work with the factory to take action when defects appear to stop problems from spreading through the full production run. // Knowing how to set clear expectations and respond quickly to issues // Challenges With Quality Control
--- slide 10: Key Takeaways // Quality control in China requires active oversight // Onsite supervision greatly reduces risk // Partnering with experts can ensure consistent quality
