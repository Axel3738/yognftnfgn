--- slide 3: Skincare Compliance & Testing Overview // FDA Registration:  Facility registration + product listing (both required) GMPC Certification: Mandatory Confirms cosmetics manufacturer follows quality, safety, and hygiene standards during production. Required Testing: Microbiology: ensures no harmful bacteria Stability: product holds up over time (shelf life) Challenge Test: preservative efficacy Compatibility: packaging doesn't react with formula Claims Compliance:  Work with a compliance company to vet ALL claims Country of Origin:  Labeling required on every unit
  NOTES: Emphasize that the manufacturer needs GMPC ‚Äî don't assume they have it. Ask for the cert. Testing should happen BEFORE you ship. Claims are where most brands get in trouble ‚Äî "anti-aging" and "acne" can cross into drug claim territory.
--- slide 4: Supplements Compliance & Testing Overview // FDA Facility Registration:  Required for any supplement manufacturer NDI Notifications:  Needed for new dietary ingredients cGMP Certification:  21 CFR Part 111 (non-negotiable) Required Testing: Microbiology: Pathogen screening Heavy Metals: Lead, mercury, arsenic, cadmium Identity Testing: Verify ingredients are what they claim Potency/Assay: confirms label accuracy COA (Certificate of Analysis):  Third-party tested, batch-specific Claims:  Structure/function claims ONLY (not drug claims) Country of Origin:  Labeling required
  NOTES: Structure/function claims = "supports immune health." Drug claims = "prevents colds." That one word difference can trigger FDA action. Stress heavy metals testing ‚Äî this is where cheap manufacturers cut corners.
--- slide 5: Electronics Compliance & Testing Overview // FCC Certification:  Required for many electronic device sold in the US UL Listing:  Safety certification, often required by Amazon & major retailers CE Marking:  Required for EU market entry Product Safety Testing: Drop tests Battery safety (lithium-ion = extra scrutiny) Electrical safety / EMC testing Country of Origin: Labeling required
  NOTES: FCC is non-negotiable ‚Äî selling without it is a federal violation. UL is technically voluntary but Amazon and Walmart increasingly require it. Lithium-ion batteries = extra testing requirements and shipping restrictions.
--- slide 6: Amazon-Specific Compliance // UPC/GTIN codes: Required for every listing Safety documentation uploads: MSDS sheets, test reports, certificates FDA letters:  Required for supplement listings Category-specific ungating: Some categories require approval + docs Consequences of non-compliance: Listing suppression Account suspension Permanent ban // Amazon can request compliance docs at ANY time– have them ready
  NOTES: Amazon doesn't warn you ‚Äî they suppress first, ask questions later. Have a compliance folder ready to go. If you can't produce documents within 48 hours, you're in trouble. Especially true during Q4 when enforcement ramps up.
--- slide 7: Recommended 3rd Party Testing Labs // SGS  A global leader, offices in China, comprehensive testing Intertek Strong in consumer goods and electronics, great for FCC/UL Eurofins Excellent for supplements and skincare, strong in micro & chemistry Pro Tips Get testing done BEFORE shipping ‚ not after it lands Budget $2K - 10K+ depending on category and scope Request quotes from multiple labs ‚ pricing varies significantly
  NOTES: We recommend getting testing done at the source in China when possible ‚Äî SGS and Intertek both have labs there. Faster and cheaper than testing stateside. If something fails, you haven't already paid for shipping.
--- slide 8: FDA Registration Certificate: Verify facility number on FDA database GMPC Certificate: Confirm it's current and covers your product type FCC Certificate: Match the FCC ID to your specific product COA (Certificate of Analysis): Batch-specific, from a third-party lab UL Listing Mark: Verify the UL file number on UL's website
  NOTES: Walk through each certificate. Show people what a real one looks like vs. a fake. Teach them to verify ‚Äî FDA has a public database, UL has a public database. If your manufacturer can't produce these, that's a red flag.
--- slide 9: Manufacturer Agreements Overview // Establish certain clauses in your Manufacturer Service Agreement (MSA) Release Forms (Brand Authorization) Authorizes manufacturer to use YOUR branding on products Required for customs clearance NDAs (Non-Disclosure Agreements) Protect proprietary designs, formulas, and processes Include a non-compete clause MSAs (Master Services Agreements) Payment terms and schedules Quality standards and inspection rights IP ownership and protection Raw material sourcing requirements // Get these signed BEFORE production starts - not after.
  NOTES: This is where most new brands get burned. They place an order, pay a deposit, and never sign anything. Then the manufacturer sells their formula to someone else, or ships garbage quality. Agreements protect you. They're not optional.
--- slide 10: Quality Control & Payment Terms // Quality Control Inspection procedures  (Pre-production/In-line/Final) Defect tolerances: AQL levels Remediation actions when quality fails Waivers & Liability Production waivers (skincare): Brand assumes responsibility Liability waivers - read carefully: Understand what you're signing // Payment Structures Deposit (typically 30%): Milestone payments Final payment AFTER inspection Never pay 100% upfront, ever Lead Times: Prototype timelines Production schedules Shipment deadlines Get it in writing
  NOTES: The payment structure is your leverage. If you pay everything upfront, you have zero negotiating power if something goes wrong. Always hold final payment until after inspection. And ALWAYS get a third-party inspection.
