2) The big idea

In e-commerce (and in life) there are:

things you can fix,

things you can’t fix,

and things you technically can fix, but they’ll take so long they might as well be impossible.

So the game isn’t “fix everything.”
 The game is fix the right thing first based on the easy-to-hard ratio.

3) The “ideal business” baseline (what good looks like)

Let’s use the example you walked through:

AOV: $64

CAC: $74

That’s a 0.86 ROAS (64 / 74)

And here’s the point:
 You can run a 0.86 all day if the rest of the model is healthy.

Most brands lose because they’re obsessed with ROAS…
 and they ignore the only question that matters:

“Do I make contribution profit within a reasonable payback window?”

4) What’s achievable vs what’s fantasy
Scenario A: “My CAC is worse than this”

You say: “Grayson, I’m at $100 CAC with a $64 AOV.”

That’s a 0.64 ROAS.

And my answer is: Cool. That can still work.

Because if your LTV curve is strong enough, you’re still profitable by:

Month 3

Month 6

Month 12

The spreadsheet shows it. This is not opinion. This is math.

Rule of thumb:
 Try to keep yourself around 0.7 ROAS or higher as a general target.
 Not because it’s magical — because it gives you room for reality (CPM spikes, refund spikes, creative fatigue).

5) The “LTV sucks” scenario (and why it’s often fixable)

Now you say: “Grayson, my LTV is trash.”

Okay — let’s cut the LTV curve in half like you did.

And what happens?

A lot of the time… you’re still making money by Month 3.

And that tells you something important:

If your LTV is low but you’re still profitable:

You’re not “doomed.” You’re just leaving money on the table.

The fix is usually:

better offer structure

higher take rate

better pricing

better first-order economics vs repeat economics

Here’s the key lesson from your example:

It can be MORE profitable to accept a higher CAC
 if it increases LTV enough to massively improve payback.

Translation:

CAC up $10

but month 3 profit goes from like $1 to $22+

you win

That’s grown-up scaling. Not “cheap CAC” scaling.




6) “My COGS suck” scenario (also fixable)

You said: “What if my COGS are 35%?”

Yeah, that’s not great.

But even then, in your walkthrough:

you’re still making money by Month 3

So the conclusion is:

COGS is usually fixable

And the fixes are straightforward:

raise prices (most brands are underpriced)

renegotiate terms

change manufacturers

improve fulfillment costs

stop giving away margin in dumb ways

Rule:
 For most brands, 30% COGS is the upper bound you want to live at long-term.

If you’re above it:

either your pricing is too low,

or your supplier is taking you to dinner and you’re paying.

7) The hard one: Low AOV (this is where people die)

Here’s the brutal truth:

Low AOV is hard to fix

Not impossible, but hard.

Because platforms have floors:

CPMs won’t go below a certain point

CPCs won’t go below a certain point

even with great ads and a great landing page, you hit limits

So if you’re selling a $19.99 subscription, you can have:

insane margins

great product

great brand

…and still lose, because there isn’t enough “meat on the bone” to buy customers consistently.

Minimum thresholds (your rules from the video)

Absolute minimum subscription price: $29.99

Realistically: $39–$49

Under $39? Only if:

margins are insane or

it’s a very specific micro-subscription setup

Low AOV usually creates the same problem:

Your CAC doesn’t have enough room to breathe.

8) Why Month 3 / Month 6 / Month 12 matter

You drew lines at Month 3, Month 6, Month 12 because those are real-world operating windows.

They map to how businesses actually run:

cash planning

inventory cycles

ad fatigue cycles

monthly budgeting

runway decisions

We’ll go way harder on this in the next video, but for now:

Payback windows tell you how aggressive you can be

Yes, you can be profitable by Month 3…

…but that doesn’t mean you can scale infinitely.

Because scaling requires upfront capital.

9) The scaling trap (why “profitable by Month 3” still breaks people)

You asked the right question:

“If I’m making money by Month 3, can’t I scale as much as I want?”

Answer: kinda.

Here’s why not:

If you want to acquire 100,000 customers, you might need like $1.7M upfront depending on CAC.

Most people don’t have that cash sitting around.

Which is why the next video matters:

I’m going to show you how to scale hard without running out of money
 even if you don’t have $1.7M sitting in your bank account.

11) Common mistakes

Obsessing over ROAS and ignoring contribution payback

Trying to “optimize” a low AOV business instead of fixing the offer

Treating COGS as unchangeable (it’s usually not)

Refusing to raise prices because you’re emotionally attached to being cheap
