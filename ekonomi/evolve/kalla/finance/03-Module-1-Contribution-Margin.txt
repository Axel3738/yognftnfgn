Module 1: Contribution Margin
4:17
1) What this lesson covers (in plain English)

If you don’t know your contribution margin, you don’t know:

how much you can spend to acquire a customer (CAC),

whether scaling will make you rich or bankrupt you,

or why “we’re doing $1M/month” can still mean “we’re broke.”

Contribution margin is the truth serum.

2) The core concept
Contribution Margin ≠ Profit

Profit (Operating Income) = what’s left after everything gets paid (rent, payroll, software, all overhead).

Contribution Margin = what’s left after variable costs (costs that go up and down with revenue).

Why it matters: Scaling decisions are made on contribution, not “profit vibes.”

3) The formula
Contribution Margin (dollars)

Contribution Margin $ = Revenue − Variable Costs

Contribution Margin (percent)

Contribution Margin % = (Revenue − Variable Costs) / Revenue

That’s it. Everything else is just arguing about what you include as “variable.”

4) What counts as “variable costs” (the list people screw up)

These are costs that increase as revenue increases.

The obvious ones

COGS (Fully loaded)
 Not “raw materials.”
 Think: cost to get the product delivered to the customer (product + packaging + fulfillment + shipping, if that’s how you run your accounting).

Ad Spend
 If you spend more to get more customers, that’s variable. Simple.

The ones that quietly steal your margin

Payment processing fees
 Shopify Payments is commonly ~2.8% + $0.30 (varies).
 Round to ~3% if you’re moving fast.

Subscription / app fees (“sub-x fees”)
 Seals / Skio / Loop / etc. often take ~0.5%–1%.
 That sounds small until you do the math:

1% of $1,000,000 = $10,000/month
 That’s a salary. Or two. Every month. Forever.

Variable team / agency costs tied to spend
 If someone gets paid % of ad spend, that is variable.

Spend $1,000,000 → 5% = $50,000

Spend $100,000 → 5% = $5,000
 It moves with spend, so it belongs in contribution margin.

5) The advanced part (where this becomes a cheat code)
First order vs. repeat orders have different cost stacks

CAC (ad spend) is typically tied to the first order

So are agency fees tied to ad spend

Repeat orders usually have better contribution margin because those costs disappear

This is why it can be rational to:

Lose $20 on the first order
 if you make $30 back over the next 90 days.

That’s not “copium.” That’s math.

6) Worked example (copy this into your brain)

Assume:

First order revenue: $100

Fully loaded COGS delivered: $35

Processing: 3% + $0.30 ≈ $3.30

Subscription platform fee: 1% = $1.00

CAC (ad spend): $40

Agency fee: 5% of ad spend = $2.00

First order contribution margin

Variable costs = 35 + 3.30 + 1.00 + 40 + 2 = $81.30
 Contribution Margin $ = 100 − 81.30 = $18.70
 Contribution Margin % = 18.70 / 100 = 18.7%

Repeat order contribution margin (no CAC, no % ad-spend fee)

Variable costs = 35 + 3.30 + 1.00 = $39.30
 Contribution Margin $ = 100 − 39.30 = $60.70
 Contribution Margin % = 60.7%

Now the “Lose money to make money” scenario

If CAC jumps to $70:

First order CM = 100 − (35 + 3.30 + 1.00 + 70 + 3.50)
 (agency is 5% of 70 = 3.50)
 = 100 − 112.80 = –$12.80

But if the average customer makes one repeat order in the next 90 days:

90-day contribution = –12.80 + 60.70 = $47.90

That’s the entire game.

7) Quick checklist (what to do in your business today)

Step 1: Pick your default “variable cost stack” for orders

COGS (fully loaded)

Processing fees

Subscription/app fees

Returns/refunds allowance (if you want to be conservative)

Step 2: Separate first order costs vs. repeat order costs

First order: include CAC + any % of ad spend fees

Repeat: remove CAC + those spend-tied fees

Step 3: Calculate:

First Order Contribution Margin $

Repeat Order Contribution Margin $

Then ask: “How many repeat orders (or how much time) until the first-order loss is paid back?”

Step 4: Decide your payback window (you referenced ~3 months)
 That becomes your operating rule for scaling.

8) Common mistakes (aka why people go broke at scale)

Calling “profit” and “contribution margin” the same thing

Ignoring processing fees (death by 3%)

Ignoring subscription platform fees (death by 1%)

Forgetting variable agency/team costs tied to spend

Using raw COGS instead of delivered COGS (lying to yourself)

Averaging everything and missing that first-order economics are different than repeats

9) Quiz (with answers)

What’s the difference between profit and contribution margin?
 Answer: Profit is after all overhead; contribution margin is after variable costs only.

Write the contribution margin formula.
 Answer: CM$ = Revenue − Variable Costs. CM% = CM$ / Revenue.

Name 5 variable costs most ecom brands should include.
 Answer: Fully loaded COGS, ad spend (CAC), processing fees, subscription/app fees, variable agency/team fees tied to spend.

Why do repeat orders usually have higher contribution margin than first orders?
 Answer: Because CAC (and spend-tied fees) usually only apply to the first order.

When is it rational to have negative contribution margin on the first order?
Answer: When the modeled contribution profit over the payback window (ex: 90 days) exceeds the initial loss with a safety buffer.
