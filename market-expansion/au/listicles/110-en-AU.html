YOUR GRATES LOOK CLEAN. THEY AREN'T.

You scrubbed the grates last Saturday. They looked good afterwards. You put the brush back, dropped the lid and didn't think about it again.

 

But if you'd lifted that lid and really looked, not at the surface, but down in the gaps between the bars, you'd have seen it. Old baked-on grease. Dark. Sticky. Hard as concrete in places. Not from last week. From last season. Maybe the season before that.

 

As a BBQ technician I've opened thousands of barbecues that look clean from the outside. The first thing I check is never the burners. It's the grates. And what I find there gets me every time. Barbecues worth $1,500, $2,200, $3,000, with grates that look like the inside of a deep fryer nobody has cleaned in three years.

 

A surface clean isn't enough. It never reaches down into the gaps where the grease actually sits. And that grease does more damage than you think.

THAT GREASE IS WRECKING THE FLAVOUR OF EVERYTHING YOU COOK

Every time you fire up the BBQ, the old grease heats up all over again. It melts. It smokes. It rises through the grates and puts its flavour into the meat you've just laid down.

 

That's the off-taste you've never been able to put your finger on. It isn't the meat, you buy good meat. It isn't the gas. It isn't the temperature. It's every cook you've done in the last three years, still sitting in the gaps, sabotaging the result you've been working towards.

 

And it doesn't stop at flavour. Old grease insulates the surface between the meat and the grates and blocks the direct contact that gives you that perfect sear. The meat sticks. The smoke smells wrong. The crust never comes out even.

 

You do everything right, and the grates sabotage it before the meat has even landed.

YOUR BRUSH IS MAKING IT WORSE — IN TWO WAYS

Most brushes people keep at home fail for one of two reasons: they either scratch away at the surface, or they take twenty minutes and still don't do the job. You've been there. Your arms ache. Your hands smell of grease. The grates look alright, and the flavour is exactly the same next time you cook.

 

So you've tried leaning on the brush harder. And yes, that works. But that extra pressure sharply increases the risk of the brush shedding its bristles straight onto the grates.

 

Weber admits it themselves, in writing, on their own support page. Thin metal wires you can't see against dark grates. Wires that lodge in the meat. That go down with it. That you serve to your family.

 

Harriet from Newcastle thought she had an ordinary stomach ache. Twelve centimetres of her small intestine were removed in hospital. Weber recalled 3.2 million brushes in February 2026 for exactly this. Your brush is probably still hanging off the BBQ right now.

Replace the brush once and for all

BUT THE WORST OF IT ISN'T THE FLAVOUR — IT'S WHAT THE GREASE DOES TO YOUR BBQ

This is the part nobody talks about.

 

The grease you leave sitting there does more than wreck the flavour. Baked-on grease is corrosive. It eats the metal slowly, from underneath. What starts as discolouration carries on as rust. And rust ends with grates flaking apart and burners seizing up.

 

A Napoleon should last you the better part of two decades. Dirty grates can take years off that. You put something like $2,200 into that BBQ. You spent time picking the right model, the right size, the right burners. And the only thing standing between that investment and a rusted-out heap is what you do with the grates after every cook.

 

It isn't only about flavour. It's about protecting something you spent weeks hunting down and thousands of dollars buying.
