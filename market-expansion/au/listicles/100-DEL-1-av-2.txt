        >
          <p>You bought good meat. Got the temperature right. Rested it afterwards. Did everything by the book.</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>And still there's always something. An off-flavour you can't quite put your finger on. A crust that never comes out properly, not like the restaurant, not like the neighbour who always seems to nail it.</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>Grandad heard that sentence his whole working life. And every time, the answer was the same.</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>It's not the meat. It's the grates.</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>Burnt-on fat from the last cook sinks down into the gaps between the grate bars. When you put the meat on, that old fat heats up again, smokes, puts its own flavour into everything and insulates the surface between the meat and the metal. That direct contact that gives you the perfect crust? Gone.</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>The Journal of Food Science confirmed it in 2024: burnt-on fat on the grates degrades the flavour compounds in the meat by up to 34 per cent. That's not a small detail. That's the difference between <i>nice</i> and <i>this is the best thing I've ever eaten</i>.</p>
